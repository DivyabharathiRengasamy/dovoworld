Shopify SEO Problems: What Holds Stores Back and How SEO Services Fix It

18 NOV 2026 · 9 MIN READ · SEO

Written by Divyabharathi, AI Automation Engineer & SEO Specialist

Quick answer: The most common Shopify SEO problems are thin collection pages, internal links to duplicate product URLs, app code slowing the store, overlapping Product schema, duplicate or missing meta descriptions, broken links after deleted products, and half-finished international setups. Shopify SEO services find these issues, fix them at the template level and track the effect on revenue.

Most Shopify stores that struggle in search are not missing anything dramatic. They are held back by a handful of small, repeated problems that build up as products are added, apps are installed and themes are customised.

This post walks through the eight problems we see most often, how to spot each one yourself, and how Shopify SEO services fix them. For a full view of what the work covers, see what Shopify SEO services include.

Eight Shopify SEO Problems and How They Are Fixed

1. Collection Pages That Are Only a Product Grid

The problem: Collection pages target the broad searches that bring the most traffic, yet many show only products with no text. Search engines have little to understand, and shoppers get no guidance.

How to spot it: Open your top collections. If there is no introduction, no buying advice and no links to related collections, the page is likely underperforming.

The fix: Add a short introduction above the grid, a helpful section below it, and links to related collections and guides, all built into the collection template so every collection can use it.

2. Internal Links to Duplicate Product URLs

The problem: Many themes link to products through their collection, producing addresses like /collections/sale/products/linen-shirt. The canonical tag points to /products/linen-shirt, but Google still has to crawl every duplicate version.

How to spot it: Hover over a product in a collection grid. If the link includes /collections/ before /products/, your theme is creating duplicates.

The fix: Update the theme's product links to use the main product URL, so crawlers and link signals go straight to the canonical page.

3. Apps Slowing Down Every Page

The problem: Reviews, pop-ups, upsells, chat and tracking apps each add scripts. Apps that were removed months ago can still leave code in the theme.

How to spot it: Run your home, collection and product pages through PageSpeed Insights and look at the list of third-party scripts. Compare it with the apps you actually use.

The fix: Remove unused apps and leftover snippets, load essential scripts only where they are needed, and replace heavy apps with native Shopify features where possible.

4. Overlapping or Incomplete Product Schema

The problem: The theme outputs Product schema, and an SEO app or review app adds another version. Conflicting prices, ratings or availability make it harder for Google to show rich results.

How to spot it: Test a product page in Google's Rich Results Test. More than one Product item, or warnings about missing fields, signals a problem.

The fix: Keep one accurate source of Product schema, include brand, reviews and identifiers such as GTIN where available, and switch off the duplicates.

5. Default Titles and Missing Meta Descriptions

The problem: Shopify themes create title tags automatically, and meta descriptions are not generated for you. Large catalogues end up with repetitive titles and blank descriptions.

How to spot it: Search site:yourstore.com on Google and scan the results, or check Search Console for pages with high impressions and low click-through.

The fix: Set title and description patterns in the templates, then write custom versions for the collections and products that earn the most impressions.

6. Broken Links and Missing Redirects After Product Changes

The problem: When a product is deleted or its handle changes, old links and any rankings it had lead to a 404 page.

How to spot it: Check Search Console for "Not found (404)" pages and crawl the store for broken internal links.

The fix: Redirect discontinued products to the closest replacement or collection using Shopify's URL redirects, and build a simple process so redirects are added whenever products are removed.

7. Incomplete International Setup

The problem: Stores selling abroad often add markets but leave translations unfinished, mix currencies or use custom hreflang code alongside the tags Shopify Markets already generates.

How to spot it: Visit each market version of a product. Look for English text on translated pages and check the page source for duplicate hreflang tags.

The fix: Choose one hreflang method, complete translations for key pages, and confirm each market uses the right domain, subfolder, language and currency.

8. Risky robots.txt Edits

The problem: Shopify lets stores customise robots.txt through a robots.txt.liquid template. Shopify warns that incorrect use "can result in loss of all traffic", and copied rules from forums often block more than intended.

How to spot it: Visit yourstore.com/robots.txt and look for custom Disallow lines you do not recognise.

The fix: Keep Shopify's default rules, add only tested directives that serve a clear purpose, and check the result in Search Console.

Frequently Asked Questions About Shopify SEO Problems

Why is my Shopify store not ranking?

Common reasons include thin collection pages, missing meta descriptions, slow pages caused by apps, duplicate schema and pages excluded from Google's index. A Search Console review and a site crawl usually reveal the main causes.

Does Shopify create duplicate content?

Shopify can create duplicate product URLs through collection paths, but it adds canonical tags that point to the main product URL. Updating theme links to use the canonical address reduces wasted crawling.

How do I speed up my Shopify store for SEO?

Remove unused apps and leftover code, compress images, limit custom fonts and third-party scripts, and choose a well-built theme. Measure Core Web Vitals before and after each change.

How do I fix 404 errors in Shopify?

Use Shopify's URL redirects to send old product or page URLs to the closest current page, and add redirects whenever you delete products or change handles.

Should I hire someone for Shopify SEO?

Small stores can handle titles, descriptions and basic content themselves. Template fixes, app clean-up, schema, international setup and robots.txt changes usually benefit from Shopify SEO services.

Do Shopify SEO apps fix everything?

No. Apps can help with tasks such as meta tags or schema, but they cannot write useful collection content, fix theme links or decide which apps to remove. Some also add duplicate schema.

Is it safe to edit robots.txt on Shopify?

It is possible through robots.txt.liquid, but Shopify warns that mistakes can remove a store from search. Only make tested changes with a clear purpose.
