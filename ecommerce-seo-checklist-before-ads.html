Ecommerce SEO checklist: 12 fixes to make before you spend more on ads

24 OCT 2026 · 6 MIN READ · SEO

Paid traffic is the fastest way to find out your store has leaks. Before you raise ad budgets, fix the problems that make every visitor, paid or organic, harder to win. This is the checklist we start with on ecommerce stores.

Get found: crawling and indexing

  • 1. Check what Google has actually indexed. In Search Console, open the Pages report and compare indexed pages with the products and categories you want found. Large gaps are the first thing to investigate.
  • 2. Control filter and parameter URLs. Sorting, colour and size filters can create thousands of near-duplicate URLs. Decide which are worth indexing and keep the rest from competing with your main category pages.
  • 3. Make canonicals, sitemaps and internal links agree. Your sitemap should list only the pages you want indexed, and canonical tags should point to the same versions.
  • 4. Do not block what Google needs. Check that robots.txt is not stopping Google from loading the scripts and styles it needs to render your pages.

Load fast on a real phone

  • 5. Measure Core Web Vitals on real pages. Look at Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint and Cumulative Layout Shift for your top product and category pages, using field data in Search Console where available.
  • 6. Audit third-party scripts. Chat widgets, review apps, tracking pixels and popups add weight. Keep what earns its place, defer what does not, and remove the rest.
  • 7. Size and serve images properly. Oversized hero and product images are one of the most common causes of slow pages.

Make product pages worth ranking

  • 8. Write titles and descriptions for shoppers. Use the product name, the key attribute people search for and a reason to click. Avoid supplier text copied across hundreds of stores.
  • 9. Add accurate Product structured data. Price, availability and currency should match what is on the page. Only mark up reviews that are real and shown on the page.
  • 10. Decide what happens to out-of-stock and discontinued products. Keep, redirect to a close alternative, or remove with intent. Leaving dead pages to pile up wastes crawl attention and frustrates shoppers.

Prove it pays

  • 11. Set up GA4 ecommerce events. View item, add to cart, begin checkout and purchase should all be tracked, so you can see revenue from organic search, not just visits.
  • 12. Connect Search Console to GA4. Seeing queries next to landing-page revenue shows which pages deserve more work.

Selling into more than one country?

Currency, language, tax display and delivery promises change what shoppers expect and what Google can show them. If you serve several markets, review how each version of your store is set up for search, and use correct language and region annotations where you run separate versions.

How to use this list

Work top to bottom. Indexing and speed problems usually cost the most, and they are cheaper to fix than buying traffic that lands on a slow or unfindable page. After the fixes, track organic revenue for a few months before judging the result, because search takes time.
